# Photographing a Hotel Bedroom and Bathroom: Bringing Both Spaces into One Frame

Before booking a hotel room, a traveller wants to understand what to expect. The bed and decoration provide an initial impression, while the bathroom shows the facilities and arrangement. When these two spaces can appear together, a carefully composed photograph helps viewers understand their relationship at a glance.

This framing involves choosing which information to bring together and how much prominence to give it. The bed needs to remain recognisable, the basin must be readable within its setting, and the boundary between the areas should be clear. A mirror can also provide complementary information about the shower. The photograph becomes a view of the layout, useful for discovering the room before looking more closely at the other images.

## Starting with what a prospective guest needs to understand

The photograph presented here was taken in a hotel bedroom. The images were intended to help rent out the rooms. I wanted a prospective guest to see the configuration within one photograph, which I consider ideal for an initial understanding of the arrangement.

My aim was therefore to bring the bedroom and bathroom into the same frame. The bed appears on the left, the basin on the right, and the glazed opening remains visible between them. Viewers have several references for locating these features in relation to one another.

In my [photography for places to stay](/en/residential-hospitality/), that understanding accompanies the presentation of atmosphere. A photograph can show colours and decoration while also explaining how a room is arranged. Here, the relationship between the sleeping area and bathroom is the main information this view provides, giving the guest a clear starting point for exploring the room visually.

## Working with a layout that made the view possible

I chose my position so I could see both the bedroom and bathroom. The room’s arrangement made this framing possible. It offered an opportunity to bring the two areas together from a single viewpoint, with enough presence for each to be understood.

This is a useful situation in interior photography: the organisation of the room can itself provide the structure of the picture. The vertical division visible beside the basin marks a boundary, while the opening towards the bedroom explains their proximity. The frame brings that information together without asking viewers to reconstruct it from photographs taken separately.

I begin with the opportunity the space offers. The camera position can be assessed by what it connects: part of the bed, the bathroom and the references that locate them together. The framing makes an existing relationship within the interior visible. That relationship is worth showing when presenting the room, particularly when it can be understood within one overall image.

## Giving the bed and basin clear places in the picture

The bed occupies a large part of the foreground. Its presence immediately identifies the bedroom, even though this view does not attempt to show every side of it. On the right, the basin, tap and mirror form another readily recognisable group.

Keeping these two visual starting points in one photograph is valuable. If either area became too secondary, the image would explain less of the intended configuration. Their relative prominence helps present their coexistence, with the division between them providing a reference within the composition.

Differences in decoration reinforce that reading. Orange walls and white bed linen appear on the bedroom side, while pale tiles and their grid identify the bathroom. These visible features help distinguish the areas. They accompany the framing, whose purpose remains to show how the spaces appear together from this viewpoint. Each area has its own visual identity while belonging to the same presentation of the room.

The division also remains a useful reference within this view. It helps explain that the facilities occupy a distinct area beside the bed. The photograph shows their proximity while allowing each part to remain identifiable, with its own surfaces and arrangement. This boundary contributes to understanding how the two spaces relate.

## Using the mirror to include the shower

The framing was deliberately designed to show the shower in the mirror. That reflection was intentional. It provides information about a facility outside the direct view, while keeping the basin and bedroom within the photograph.

The mirror shows the shower above the basin, within a clearly identifiable boundary. Viewers can distinguish the objects directly in front of them from the area presented through reflection. This supplements the information provided by the basin: several bathroom facilities can be understood together within the picture.

That use of the mirror builds on the questions raised by [reflections in bathroom photography](/en/post/bathroom-photography-mastering-reflections-tight-spaces/), with a specific intention. Here, the reflection shows more of the layout from the chosen camera position. The shower visible in the mirror contributes directly to the purpose of the photograph: providing a shared understanding of the bedroom and bathroom. It adds useful information while the overall arrangement remains readable from a single viewpoint.

## Preparing the photograph with the client

I carried out some light staging with the client before taking the photograph. This preparation was part of presenting the room. In a view bringing several areas together, all the visible elements contribute to the overall impression and to how easily the place can be understood.

That preparation needs to be considered across the whole frame. The eye moves from the bed towards the bathroom, but also into the mirror. Surfaces and objects seen directly or through reflection therefore belong to the same picture, even when they lie in different directions from the camera.

The photograph was taken a long time ago, and I no longer remember particular technical choices. What I retain is the intention behind the framing, the role of the layout and the deliberate inclusion of the shower in the reflection. Those decisions explain the image without attributing precise settings to a shoot whose technical details I do not recall.

## Completing the layout view with other scales of observation

Other wide-angle photographs and tighter views accompanied this image. It provided a combined reading of the two spaces, while the complementary pictures continued the discovery of the room. The arrangement can be understood here; the series then offers other ways to look at the same interior.

That relationship between images matters when presenting accommodation. An overall photograph locates facilities in relation to one another. Closer framing provides another scale of observation.
