A fitness room is one of the facilities a traveller may consider before booking a hotel. Photographs help guests identify the equipment available and picture where they could exercise during their stay. Preparing the room deserves a place in the planning of a hotel photography project.

In this photograph, the cardio and strength training equipment, dumbbells, screens and framed posters create a setting with many details. Each may need attention before the shoot begins.

During my [hotel photography projects](/en/residential-hospitality/), I aim to plan these steps with the property’s team. A suitable time slot, an identified contact and a prepared room allow the session to focus on the photographs while taking the hotel’s daily operations into account.

## Establish Which Equipment and Services the Images Should Present

Before the shoot, a conversation with the hotel clarifies what the fitness room actually offers. Which machines can guests use? Are the smaller accessories part of the regular provision? Is any equipment due to be replaced, or is the room about to be rearranged?

This information shapes the shot list. If a machine will soon be replaced, it helps to decide in advance whether the shoot can wait until its replacement is installed or whether additional photography will be needed later.

Preparation also covers associated services. Towels, water or exercise mats can appear when they reflect what guests are normally offered. Their presence in a photograph creates an expectation for the stay.

I also distinguish what photographs can communicate from the information the hotel needs to supply in writing. Opening hours, access arrangements and any booking requirements belong in the description of the facility. This initial conversation gives the project a clear scope and helps avoid discovering significant changes on the day.

## Coordinate Access with Reception and the Team on Site

A fitness room remains a space used by hotel guests. The choice of time should therefore reflect the patterns of use known to the hotel, together with the availability of the team responsible for preparing it.

Reception needs to know when the shoot will take place and what access arrangements have been agreed. If a temporary closure is necessary, the hotel can organise it and inform guests. A contact on site can help manage a change of plan or an unexpected arrival.

It is also useful to gather whatever is needed to enter the room and operate its controls: a key card, a key, lighting controls or the television remote. Having these ready avoids involving several people while photography is under way.

Coordination with cleaning matters just as much as the photographer’s arrival time. The room should be ready at the start of the slot. This extends the work of [scouting before a photo shoot](/en/post/importance-scouting-before-architecture-real-estate-shoot/) to the daily constraints of an occupied hotel.

## Clean the Surfaces That Will Appear in the Photographs

Visual preparation begins with the areas that will appear in the images: flooring, seats, handles, consoles and equipment surfaces. Marks and dust on dark or reflective materials can become particularly noticeable depending on the direction of the light.

The team can schedule this work close enough to the shoot for the room to remain in the expected condition. Surfaces should have time to dry, and cleaning equipment should be put away before the photography setup begins.

The areas around the machines also deserve attention. A small section behind a bench may appear from a viewpoint that was not obvious during an initial walk through. Skirting boards, floor corners and equipment bases then become part of the overall impression.

I suggest finishing this stage by looking from the main positions planned for the photographs. This helps identify any remaining preparation needed before work on the series begins, while the team is still available to make those final adjustments.

## Give Dumbbells and Accessories a Clear Place

Smaller items need a different kind of attention from the machines. Dumbbells, mats and strength training accessories may be easy to recognise individually, but become confusing when too many overlap within an overall view.

In the photograph shown here, the dumbbells are readily identifiable. Their position is one of the details to review during preparation, just as the position of a bench or chair would be in another interior.

The aim is to restore the room’s usual arrangement after use. Accessories return to their designated places, sets are grouped together and circulation routes remain clear. This organisation helps explain what is available and where each item belongs.

I make these adjustments with someone who knows how the room operates. Heavy machines remain in their normal positions. If a piece of equipment obscures part of the room, an additional view can provide the missing information without changing the layout solely for the photograph. This also keeps the presentation consistent with the space guests will find.

## Check the Lighting, Television and Equipment Consoles

In an equipped fitness room, several screens may operate alongside the ceiling lighting. Their presentation should be agreed before taking a series of photographs.

In this image, the television appears as a dark surface while the cardio machine’s console is illuminated. These two situations raise different questions: what should each screen contribute to the presentation, and how much attention should it attract?

A television can remain off when its content adds no useful information. If it is switched on, the programme displayed should suit the hotel’s presentation and remain consistent between views. For equipment consoles, a team member can explain the display options and standby behaviour.

Lighting controls should also be accessible. I check their effect in the photograph, together with reflections on screens and equipment. This establishes a starting configuration and reduces interruptions during the shoot. It also allows the team to clarify which settings are normal for the room before photography begins.

## Plan the Views Before Rearranging the Room

When several machines occupy a limited space, sharing a short shot list with the hotel is useful. An overall view can introduce the room, followed by complementary photographs showing equipment that is less visible in the main image.

This list helps the team concentrate preparation on the relevant areas. It also makes it possible to allow space for the camera setup and for movement during the session.

The photographer’s position may be enough to resolve an overlap between two machines. Checking this before moving accessories avoids unnecessary handling and helps maintain a consistent arrangement from one photograph to the next.

Composition and the presentation of different uses are explored in my article on [photographing a gym](/en/post/photographing-gym-space/). Here, the preparation task is to make the planned views achievable within the available time, with an arrangement that remains faithful to the room guests will actually use during their stay.

## Make a Final Check and Plan the Return to Normal Use

Before taking the first photographs, a final walk through with the hotel contact provides an opportunity to confirm the essentials:

- Reception knows the time slot and the agreed access arrangements.
- Cleaning is complete and cleaning equipment has been put away.
- Accessories are in place and circulation routes are clear.
- Lights and screens are set as agreed for the shoot.
- The priority shot list has been shared.

At the end of the session, any temporary adjustments are reversed with the team. Controls, accessories and access to the room return to their usual arrangement. Time for this should be included in the schedule.
