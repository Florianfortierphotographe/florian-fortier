# Photographing a Façade with Staggered Heights: Making the Building’s Composition Clear

A façade composed of several heights cannot be understood simply by counting its windows. Its design also depends on the differences between the building’s parts, the lines connecting them and the position of upper levels set further back. Photographing it involves showing that arrangement while giving each part an appropriate place within the overall view.

This makes the establishing image particularly valuable. It allows viewers to compare heights, follow changes in the outline and understand how materials contribute to the design. In my architecture photography, I look for a frame that brings this information together clearly. Viewpoint, the boundaries of the picture and lighting all need to support the same reading of the building.

## Starting with the architecture to be showcased

For the assignment presented here, I was working to showcase a newly built property development. My intention was to show the architecture and design of the building, including the materials used. The photograph therefore needed to present an overall composition, with windows, balconies and surface treatments visible in relation to one another.

In this view, the left-hand part rises higher and includes several balconies stacked above one another. On the right, horizontal lines mark the changes in height, with the upper parts visible further back. These differences define the building’s outline. Keeping them together helps explain how they relate to each other.

This approach forms part of my [architecture photography](/en/architecture/). I begin with the project features that need to be shown, then decide what must remain visible. Here, the variation in heights and surfaces called for an image in which the different parts of the façade could be compared within a single view.

## Choosing a parallel position for a harmonious composition

I chose to position myself parallel to the façade because I found it produced a more harmonious composition. From that position, the openings, balconies and horizontal lines form relationships that are easy to follow. The eye can move from the taller part on the left towards the more extended section on the right.

This choice does not imply that the building is symmetrical. Its two sides have different heights and different designs. The value of this position is precisely that it brings those differences into a stable picture, giving each part enough presence. The harmony comes from their relationship within the frame, without requiring an identical arrangement on either side.

The image also retains the setbacks visible in the upper parts. Their boundaries remain distinct from the surfaces in front of them. This allows the overall outline to be presented while preserving clues to depth. The viewpoint helps clarify the building’s composition, including its changes in height and the shifts between nearer and more distant planes.

## Keeping the complete façade to make heights comparable

I framed the photograph to include the entire façade, filling the picture with the building. Its boundaries matter because they allow viewers to read the difference between the highest part and the horizontal lines extending across the right-hand side. Cutting off the top or an outer edge would have removed useful information from this overall view.

In the photograph, the sky separates the upper outline from its background, while the ground locates the base of the building. Neighbouring structures appear at the sides, around the edge of the main subject. The façade therefore retains a strong presence while remaining connected to its visible surroundings.

Filling the frame is a question of the prominence given to the subject. Enough space needs to remain around its contours for the boundaries to be clear, while the building must appear large enough for the arrangement of openings and differences in surfaces to be seen. That relationship between an overall view and visible information gives the main photograph its purpose.

## Reading materials at the scale of the whole building

Materials were among the features I wanted to showcase. In this photograph, their presence comes across through tones, surfaces and the visible design of the façade. The light areas, dark base and balustrades create several references that accompany the reading of the different heights.

The repeated curved motifs on the right also give the façade a rhythm. They work alongside the alignment of the windows. On the left, the sequence of balconies creates a different arrangement. The overall view allows these two treatments to be considered together, with each contributing to the appearance of the building.

My interest here is the role materials play in the complete composition. A detail photograph would offer a different scale of observation. For this image, the priority is to show how surfaces and patterns contribute to the building’s design. This extends the question of a [façade’s architectural identity](/en/post/photographing-facades-architectural-identity/), applying it to a building whose heights and surface treatments vary across its elevation.

## Planning for an illuminated façade and including its surroundings

I chose the time of my visit so the façade would be illuminated. That was part of preparing the assignment: I wanted the light to support the presentation of the architecture. The pale surfaces, window surrounds and balconies could then be read together across an illuminated façade.

The light helps distinguish exposed surfaces from the shaded areas visible beneath balconies or within setbacks. It provides additional references alongside the building’s lines. Framing and lighting contribute together: one brings the façade’s parts into the picture, while the other helps reveal the differences between its planes and the recesses within them.

The street lamps, planting and fencing in the foreground were not constraints in themselves, although I had to compose with their presence. They occupy part of the view and overlap the building in places. I consider them within the picture as a whole, keeping the complete façade and a clear understanding of its design as my priorities.

## Giving the main photograph a clear role in the series

This photograph belonged to a small series and was its main image. It needed to bring together the information that introduces the project: the complete façade, its staggered heights, its architecture and the presence of materials within its design.

Within a [coherent architecture photography series](/en/post/building-coherent-series-architecture-photography/), this overall image provides a framework for understanding the building. Its value lies in what can be understood immediately, without having to reconstruct the façade from several photographs. Here, it gathers the differences that make up the composition of the whole.

Photographing a façade with staggered heights means preserving the relationships between its parts. The parallel position, complete framing and choice of an illuminated façade all served that intention on this assignment.
