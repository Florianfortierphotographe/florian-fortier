# Photographing a Pool Surrounded by Mirrors: Distinguishing the Pool from Its Reflections

Mirrors can transform the way an indoor pool is perceived. They extend lines, repeat the water and reveal parts of the room in several directions. This visual richness raises a practical question for the photographer: how can the size of the pool come across without its reflections taking over the image? A photograph needs to help viewers understand what they are looking at, even when the interior produces several versions of the same feature.

I look for a balance between the presence of reflective surfaces and a clear view of the pool itself. The framing needs to show its volume, its lighting and its position within the building. Mirrors contribute to that presentation, but their prominence needs careful consideration. This decision begins with where I place the camera and what that position allows me to include.

## Establishing the pool as the main subject

For the assignment illustrated here, the brief was to showcase the swimming pool. I wanted to show its volume, its lighting and the fact that it was indoors. These three intentions give the framing a direction: enough water needs to be visible to make the pool understandable, together with elements that explain the space around it.

The mirrors add a compositional challenge because some of what appears in the picture is repeated. If too much space is given to that repetition, the image may be striking while providing less clear information about the pool. I start with the subject of the brief, then consider how the reflections support its presentation.

This approach is part of my work in [architecture and interior photography](/en/architecture/): selecting the information that helps present a place. Here, the pool provides the starting point. Furniture, openings and reflective surfaces then help viewers understand the surrounding interior. Their place in the frame needs to remain connected to the subject I was asked to photograph.

## Using the pool edges as spatial references

The edges of the pool play an important role in this photograph. They establish a boundary between the water and the surrounding floor. The edge extending into the foreground gives the eye a direction to follow, while the return at the far end helps explain the overall shape. These references remain useful where the adjoining mirror repeats similar lines.

A photograph cannot provide the actual dimensions of a pool. It can, however, suggest its spatial arrangement: how it extends through the room and how its different parts relate to one another. Continuous outlines and enough surrounding context help communicate that arrangement without requiring measurements to be inferred from the picture.

The lines visible beneath the water add another layer. In the image, they overlap with reflections on the surface. I therefore rely on several references together, including the pool boundaries and the adjacent floor. A single line can be ambiguous. A consistent set of visual clues gives viewers a better chance of understanding what is water, what surrounds it and where the reflection begins.

## Giving the wall mirror a measured presence

I chose my viewpoint to emphasise the size of the pool without including too much reflection in the mirror. The aim was to find a position from which the pool itself would retain enough presence alongside its reflected image. This was a decision about the relationship between the subject and the repeated information around it.

The mirror occupies the left side of the photograph. It repeats the water, the seating and part of the architecture. Its boundary and its meeting with the floor provide clues for distinguishing the room from its reflection. Keeping it in the frame also shows an actual feature of the interior, one that contributes strongly to how the space is experienced visually.

That balance depends on viewpoint. Moving changes which elements a reflective surface sends towards the camera and how much of the composition they occupy. The practical question becomes: what proportion of pool and what proportion of reflection best serve this view? I use the pool as my reference when assessing the balance, since showcasing it was the purpose of the assignment.

## Checking what the reflective surfaces bring into view

The mirrors were a constraint during the shoot. I had to pay attention to reflections. In this kind of interior, checking the frame means considering both the objects directly in front of the camera and the elements returned by reflective surfaces from other directions.

That involves looking across the picture: the mirrored wall, the reflective section of ceiling, the glazing and the points where these surfaces meet other features. A repeated element can appear to belong to the back of the room while actually being reflected from elsewhere. Boundaries between surfaces and breaks in otherwise continuous lines become useful information for understanding the composition.

Reflections on the water did not cause any particular difficulty for this photograph. I distinguish that from the attention required by the wall mirror. Both surfaces appear in the same image, but they did not present the same challenge on this occasion. The water retains reflections while also revealing the pattern beneath its surface. The pool within its setting remains the subject.

## Showing the lighting within an enclosed space

Lighting was one of the features I wanted to showcase. Its presence can be seen in the pool and around the room, through the light points and illuminated lines included in the frame. Showing their relationship with the architecture helps explain what the lighting contributes to this particular interior.

The ceiling, walls and large arched opening also establish that the pool is indoors. A close view of water alone would make that characteristic far less apparent. The composition therefore connects the pool with the room enclosing it, giving viewers enough context to understand the setting rather than asking them to reconstruct it from a detail.

This is a more specific question than a general presentation of materials or atmosphere in an [indoor swimming pool](/en/post/photographing-indoor-swimming-pool/). Here, my focus is how the pool remains clear when mirrors repeat both its boundaries and its surroundings. Its lighting is one of the characteristics that needs to remain visible within that arrangement.

## Giving this photograph a role in the wider series

This image belongs to a broader series. It does not have to communicate every aspect of the pool by itself. Its contribution is to bring together the pool’s volume, its lighting and its indoor setting in a composition where mirrors are visible without becoming the sole subject.

A [coherent architecture photography series](/en/post/building-coherent-series-architecture-photography/) depends on what each view contributes. For this photograph, I assess the framing against the original intention: does the pool have enough presence, is its position within the room understandable, and do the reflections support that reading? These questions keep the image connected to the brief.
